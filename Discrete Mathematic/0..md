**Discrete Mathematics Crash Course**

This crash course provides a high-level overview of essential topics in discrete mathematics, fundamental to computer science and advanced mathematics.

**Chapter 1: The Foundations: Logic and Proofs**

- **1.1 Propositional Logic:** Introduces the basic building blocks of logic: propositions (statements that are true or false), logical connectives (AND, OR, NOT, IF-THEN, IF AND ONLY IF), and truth tables to evaluate compound propositions.
    
- **1.2 Applications of Propositional Logic:** Explores practical uses of propositional logic, such as in designing logical circuits and for program verification.
    
- **1.3 Propositional Equivalences:** Covers how to show that different compound propositions have the same truth values, introducing concepts like logical equivalence and important laws (e.g., De Morgan's Laws).
    
- **1.4 Predicates and Quantifiers:** Extends propositional logic to handle statements about properties of objects and relationships between them using predicates and quantifiers (universal "for all" and existential "there exists").
    
- **1.5 Nested Quantifiers:** Deals with statements involving multiple quantifiers, crucial for expressing complex mathematical and computational ideas.
    
- **1.6 Rules of Inference:** Presents methods for drawing valid conclusions from a set of premises, forming the backbone of logical arguments and automated reasoning.
    
- **1.7 Introduction to Proofs:** Explains what constitutes a mathematical proof, common proof terminology, and the structure of direct proofs.
    
- **1.8 Proof Methods and Strategy:** Introduces various proof techniques such as direct proofs, indirect proofs (proof by contradiction, contraposition), and proof by cases, along with strategies for constructing them.
    

**Chapter 2: Basic Structures: Sets, Functions, Sequences, Sums, and Matrices**

- **2.1 Sets:** Defines sets, set notation, and basic set theory concepts like subsets, supersets, and the empty set.
    
- **2.2 Set Operations:** Discusses operations on sets such as union, intersection, complement, and difference.
    
- **2.3 Functions:** Introduces functions, their properties (one-to-one, onto), types (injective, surjective, bijective), and inverses.
    
- **2.4 Sequences and Summations:** Covers sequences (ordered lists of elements) and summations (the sum of terms in a sequence).
    
- **2.5 Cardinality of Sets:** Deals with the size of sets, including finite and infinite sets, and concepts like countable and uncountable infinities.
    
- **2.6 Matrices:** Introduces matrices, their basic operations (addition, multiplication), and their applications.
    

**Chapter 3: Algorithms**

- **3.1 Algorithms:** Defines algorithms as step-by-step procedures to solve problems, discussing their properties and common types.
    
- **3.2 The Growth of Functions:** Analyzes how the running time or space requirements of algorithms grow with input size, introducing Big-O notation.
    
- **3.3 Complexity of Algorithms:** Categorizes algorithms based on their time and space complexity, comparing their efficiency.
    

**Chapter 4: Number Theory and Cryptography**

- **4.1 Divisibility and Modular Arithmetic:** Covers concepts of divisibility, the division algorithm, and arithmetic operations within a modulus.
    
- **4.2 Integer Representations and Algorithms:** Explores different bases for representing integers and algorithms for conversions and arithmetic operations.
    
- **4.3 Primes and Greatest Common Divisors:** Discusses prime numbers, composite numbers, the fundamental theorem of arithmetic, and methods for finding GCDs and LCMs.
    
- **4.4 Solving Congruences:** Focuses on solving linear congruences and systems of congruences.
    
- **4.5 Applications of Congruences:** Shows practical applications of modular arithmetic, such as in hashing functions and pseudorandom number generation.
    
- **4.6 Cryptography:** Introduces principles of cryptography, including Caesar ciphers, public-key cryptography (like RSA), and the role of number theory in securing communications.
    

**Chapter 5: Induction and Recursion**

- **5.1 Mathematical Induction:** Explains a powerful proof technique for proving statements about all positive integers.
    
- **5.2 Strong Induction and Well-Ordering:** Introduces variations of induction and the well-ordering principle.
    
- **5.3 Recursive Definitions and Structural Induction:** Defines sequences, functions, and sets recursively, and introduces structural induction for proving properties about recursively defined structures.
    
- **5.4 Recursive Algorithms:** Covers algorithms that solve problems by reducing them to smaller instances of the same problem.
    
- **5.5 Program Correctness:** Discusses methods for proving that programs or algorithms correctly produce the desired output.
    

**Chapter 6: Counting**

- **6.1 The Basics of Counting:** Introduces fundamental counting principles like the product rule and sum rule.
    
- **6.2 The Pigeonhole Principle:** Explores this principle used for proving existence results in counting problems.
    
- **6.3 Permutations and Combinations:** Defines permutations (ordered arrangements) and combinations (unordered selections) and their formulas.
    
- **6.4 Binomial Coefficients and Identities:** Discusses binomial theorem and related identities.
    
- **6.5 Generalized Permutations and Combinations:** Extends counting techniques to scenarios with repetitions and indistinguishable items.
    
- **6.6 Generating Permutations and Combinations:** Explores algorithms for systematically listing all permutations or combinations.
    

**Chapter 7: Discrete Probability**

- **7.1 An Introduction to Discrete Probability:** Defines basic concepts of discrete probability, including sample spaces, events, and probability calculations.
    
- **7.2 Probability Theory:** Delves deeper into probability axioms, conditional probability, and independence.
    
- **7.3 Bayes’ Theorem:** Explains Bayes' theorem for updating probabilities based on new evidence.
    
- **7.4 Expected Value and Variance:** Covers measures of central tendency and dispersion for random variables.
    

**Chapter 8: Advanced Counting Techniques**

- **8.1 Applications of Recurrence Relations:** Uses recurrence relations to model and solve problems involving sequences.
    
- **8.2 Solving Linear Recurrence Relations:** Provides methods for solving various types of recurrence relations.
    
- **8.3 Divide-and-Conquer Algorithms and Recurrence Relations:** Analyzes the complexity of algorithms that break problems into smaller subproblems.
    
- **8.4 Generating Functions:** Introduces generating functions as a tool for solving recurrence relations and counting problems.
    
- **8.5 Inclusion–Exclusion:** Explains the principle of inclusion-exclusion for counting elements in overlapping sets.
    
- **8.6 Applications of Inclusion–Exclusion:** Shows how to apply the principle to various counting problems.
    

**Chapter 9: Relations**

- **9.1 Relations and Their Properties:** Defines binary relations and their properties (reflexive, symmetric, transitive, antisymmetric).
    
- **9.2 n-ary Relations and Their Applications:** Extends relations to more than two elements and discusses their uses in databases.
    
- **9.3 Representing Relations:** Shows different ways to represent relations, such as using matrices and directed graphs.
    
- **9.4 Closures of Relations:** Covers reflexive, symmetric, and transitive closures of relations.
    
- **9.5 Equivalence Relations:** Defines equivalence relations and their connection to partitions.
    
- **9.6 Partial Orderings:** Introduces partial orderings and concepts like Hasse diagrams.
    

**Chapter 10: Graphs**

- **10.1 Graphs and Graph Models:** Defines graphs as mathematical structures consisting of vertices and edges, used to model relationships.
    
- **10.2 Graph Terminology and Special Types of Graphs:** Introduces various terms (e.g., degree, path, cycle) and types of graphs (e.g., complete, bipartite).
    
- **10.3 Representing Graphs and Graph Isomorphism:** Discusses adjacency matrices, adjacency lists, and how to determine if two graphs are structurally identical.
    
- **10.4 Connectivity:** Explores connected components, paths, and cycles in graphs.
    
- **10.5 Euler and Hamilton Paths:** Covers specific types of paths that visit every edge or every vertex exactly once.
    
- **10.6 Shortest-Path Problems:** Presents algorithms (e.g., Dijkstra's algorithm) for finding the shortest path between two vertices.
    
- **10.7 Planar Graphs:** Discusses graphs that can be drawn without edges crossing.
    
- **10.8 Graph Coloring:** Introduces graph coloring problems and their applications.
    

**Chapter 11: Trees**

- **11.1 Introduction to Trees:** Defines trees as connected acyclic graphs and their basic properties.
    
- **11.2 Applications of Trees:** Explores uses of trees in various fields, such as in data structures and decision-making.
    
- **11.3 Tree Traversal:** Discusses methods for visiting all nodes in a tree (e.g., pre-order, in-order, post-order).
    
- **11.4 Spanning Trees:** Covers spanning trees of a graph and algorithms to find them.
    
- **11.5 Minimum Spanning Trees:** Focuses on finding spanning trees with the minimum possible total edge weight.
    

**Chapter 12: Boolean Algebra**

- **12.1 Boolean Functions:** Defines Boolean functions and their relationship to logic.
    
- **12.2 Representing Boolean Functions:** Shows various ways to represent Boolean functions, including truth tables and Boolean expressions.
    
- **12.3 Logic Gates:** Introduces basic logic gates (AND, OR, NOT, XOR) and how they form the basis of digital circuits.
    
- **12.4 Minimization of Circuits:** Discusses techniques (e.g., Karnaugh maps) for simplifying Boolean expressions and circuit designs.
    

**Chapter 13: Modeling Computation**

- **13.1 Languages and Grammars:** Introduces formal languages and grammars as a way to describe valid strings in a language.
    
- **13.2 Finite-State Machines with Output:** Covers finite-state machines that produce output based on their current state and input (e.g., Mealy and Moore machines).
    
- **13.3 Finite-State Machines with No Output:** Discusses finite automata used for language recognition.
    
- **13.4 Language Recognition:** Explores how finite automata can recognize regular languages.
    
- **13.5 Turing Machines:** Introduces the theoretical model of computation, the Turing machine, which forms the basis of modern computers and computability theory.
