The sixth limb of Ashtanga Yoga is **Dharana**, which translates to **concentration**.1 It is the state of mind where you make a conscious effort to focus on a single object, thought, or point.2

---

### Understanding Dharana

Dharana is the practice of **unwavering focus**, and it can be directed toward anything.3 The speaker provides several examples:

- A physical object, such as a **candle flame** or a specific symbol.4
    
- A sound or **mantra**.5
    
- A visual image, through **visualization**.6
    
- A point on your body, such as your breath or a chakra.7
    

The key element is the **effort involved**. At this stage, your mind is still prone to wandering, so you must actively and intentionally bring your focus back to the chosen point of concentration whenever it drifts.8

---

### The Role of Dharana

Dharana is considered the **stepping stone** to the next stage of yoga: Dhyana (meditation).9 You cannot achieve true meditation without first mastering concentration.

- **From Effort to Effortless:** Dharana is the practice where you learn to tame the mind.10 The initial effort to concentrate eventually becomes a natural state, which leads directly into meditation.
    
- **A Deeper State of Mind:** By focusing your attention on a single point, you quiet the constant stream of thoughts and bring your awareness inward.11 This practice is essential for experiencing the deeper, more blissful levels of consciousness.
