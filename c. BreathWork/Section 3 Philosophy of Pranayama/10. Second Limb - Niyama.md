This video explains the second limb of Ashtanga Yoga, known as **Niyama**. While the first limb, Yama, deals with social ethics and how you interact with the external world, Niyama focuses on **personal observances** and how you behave with yourself.

Niyama has five subcategories:

---

### 1. Saucha (Cleanliness)

This is not just about keeping your physical surroundings clean. Saucha refers to both **external and internal cleanliness**.

- **External Cleanliness:** Keeping your body and environment clean.
    
- **Internal Cleanliness:** Purifying your mind, body, and spirit through practices like yoga, meditation, and pranayama to overcome internal impurities and negative impressions.
    

---

### 2. Santosha (Contentment)

Santosha is about finding **true, permanent happiness** within yourself, rather than seeking temporary happiness through external desires like getting a job, a new car, or a relationship. The speaker argues that while it's not wrong to have desires, true bliss and contentment come from a state of inner satisfaction.

---

### 3. Tapas (Austerity or Penance)

Tapas refers to the willingness to **voluntarily endure discomfort** as part of a process. The speaker uses the examples of being on a long flight or struggling to hold a difficult yoga pose. It's about accepting and moving through uncomfortable situations to strengthen your resolve and grow.

---

### 4. Svadhyaya (Self-Study)

Svadhyaya means **"self-study"**. This involves two aspects:

- **Studying Scriptures:** Learning the philosophical knowledge of yoga.
    
- **Self-Introspection:** Continuously observing and studying your own thoughts, actions, and speech without judgment. This practice leads to a deeper awareness of who you are.
    

---

### 5. Ishvara Pranidhana (Surrender to the Divine)

Ishvara Pranidhana means **"surrender to the Divine"** or the Supreme Being. It involves completely surrendering your ego and recognizing that you are part of a larger consciousness or universe. It’s an act of faith and total dedication to the divine, acknowledging that everything is taken care of.

The speaker concludes by noting that Tapas, Svadhyaya, and Ishvara Pranidhana together form **Kriya Yoga** (the path of action). Tapas is the path of action, Svadhyaya is the path of knowledge (Jnana Yoga), and Ishvara Pranidhana is the path of devotion (Bhakti Yoga).
