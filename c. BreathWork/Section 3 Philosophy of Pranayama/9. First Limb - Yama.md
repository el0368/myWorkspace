The first limb of Ashtanga Yoga is **Yama** (or Yam), which focuses on **social ethics** and how you relate to the external world. It provides a moral code to guide your behavior and interactions.

There are five subcategories under Yama:

---

### 1. Ahimsa: Non-Violence

Ahimsa is more than just avoiding physical violence. According to the speaker, it extends to three levels:

- **Actions:** Do not physically harm others.
    
- **Speech:** Avoid using violent or harmful words.
    
- **Thoughts:** Do not harbor violent or hateful thoughts.
    

By practicing non-violence at all three levels, you cultivate a peaceful aura that influences those around you, making them non-violent in return.

---

### 2. Satya: Truthfulness

Satya means being truthful, not just in your speech but also in your thoughts. The speaker stresses that a pure intention behind your actions is essential for you to receive the "fruits" of your efforts. When you are truthful in thought and action, your integrity is reflected in the outcomes you achieve.

---

### 3. Asteya: Non-Stealing

Asteya goes beyond the physical act of stealing. It also applies to the mind. The speaker highlights that this includes:

- Not stealing someone else's property.
    
- Not being jealous of others' possessions or achievements.
    
- Not stealing someone's thoughts, plans, or ideas.
    

Asteya is about trusting that what is rightfully yours will always come to you, so there is no need to covet or take what belongs to others.

---

### 4. Brahmacharya: Right Use of Energy

Brahmacharya is often misunderstood as simply celibacy. The speaker clarifies that it is about the **right use of energy**. The word itself means "moving towards Brahman (infinity)." It is a path of directing your energy, especially spiritual energy, toward a higher purpose rather than squandering it on unproductive pursuits. This practice helps to purify the body and mind.

---

### 5. Aparigraha: Non-Accumulation

Aparigraha is the practice of non-attachment and non-hoarding. This applies not only to material possessions but also to emotions. The speaker refers to the accumulation of "emotional garbage" like overthinking and getting stuck in one's own mind. The secret to a happy life, according to the video, is not to accumulate but to **give** to others.
