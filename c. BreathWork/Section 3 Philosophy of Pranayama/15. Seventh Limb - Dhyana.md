The video explains **Dhyana**, the seventh limb of Ashtanga Yoga, which is widely known as **meditation**. It highlights that Dhyana is not a separate practice but an integral part of yoga that should be done alongside physical postures (asanas) and breathing techniques.

---

### The Nature of Dhyana (Meditation)

The speaker distinguishes Dhyana from the previous limb, Dharana (concentration). While Dharana requires **effort and focus**, Dhyana is described as **completely effortless**.

- **Awareness vs. Concentration**: The core of Dhyana is not concentration, but **awareness**. You don't try to force your mind to focus on a single point. Instead, you simply become aware of everything—your breath, your body, and your surroundings—without judgment. The speaker notes this is a "huge difference" from Dharana, where effort is key.
    
- **The Mind-Body Connection**: The video stresses that meditation is vital for a healthy lifestyle. While many people focus on a strong body, a weak mind is a poor combination. The goal of yoga, in its purest form, is to cultivate both a strong body and a strong mind.
    

---

### Benefits of Dhyana

According to the video, meditation has profound benefits for both the mind and body:

- **Mental Clarity and Decision-Making**: A regular meditation practice allows you to handle difficult situations more effectively and make better decisions by training your mind. It helps you navigate the distinction between right and wrong.
    
- **Stress Reduction**: Meditation lowers the level of **cortisol**, the stress hormone, which in turn benefits the cells of the entire body. The physical benefits of meditation are often a result of reduced stress.
    
- **Inner Peace**: The practice helps you cultivate a deep sense of inner peace, which allows you to deal with the external world more beautifully and with greater ease.
    

The video concludes by emphasizing that a world where everyone practices meditation would be a healthier and happier place.
