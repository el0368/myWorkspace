In this video, the speaker guides you through a breathing technique called **Kapalabhati**, also known as "shining skull breath," which is part of the "Shatkarma" method of cleansing. This is followed by a short, guided meditation.

---

### The Breathing Exercise: Kapalabhati Pranayama

This technique involves active exhalations and passive inhalations.

1. **Preparation**: Sit in a comfortable, cross-legged position. Rest your palms on your knees with an open palm position (no mudra specified, but an open position is indicated). Close your eyes, keep your spine straight, and relax your shoulders.
    
2. **The Technique**: The exercise is performed in three sets of 30 repetitions each.
    
    - **Exhalation**: Forcefully and actively exhale through your nose. As you exhale, pull your stomach inward sharply. The speaker emphasizes that this is the active part of the breath.
        
    - **Inhalation**: Inhale automatically and passively. The speaker states you don't need to make an effort to breathe in; it will happen on its own as your stomach releases.
        
3. **Rest**: After each set of 30 repetitions, relax your body and breathe normally before starting the next set.
    

---

### The Guided Meditation

After completing the three sets of Kapalabhati, the speaker guides you into a short meditation.

1. **Transition**: Keep your eyes closed. Take a long, deep breath to relax your body.
    
2. **Focus on the Throat**: Direct your attention to your throat. Become aware of the physical space there, both inside and outside. As you breathe, feel the air moving around this area.
    
3. **Visualization and Gratitude**: Imagine the empty space around your throat is a "vast blue sky." The speaker instructs you to feel a sense of gratitude for your body and life.
    
4. **Observation**: During this time, thoughts and emotions may come and go. The key is to simply let them be, without getting attached to them. The speaker reminds you that you are a witness to these thoughts, a layer "much above" them.
    
5. **Conclusion**: After a period of silence and observation, gently become aware of your body, breath, and surroundings. When you feel comfortable, slowly open your eyes.
