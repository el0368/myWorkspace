### The Two Phases of Recursion: The Core Takeaway ↕️

The instructor concludes by summarizing the most important lesson from the examples and analogies. In his words, "the main important thing that I have to show you here in the recursion is that **recursion has two phases. One is calling phase and the other one is returning phase.**"

He also describes these phases with another set of terms: "**ascending and then descending.** You are going to next, next, next, next ascending. Then you came back and back and back. So descending."

The critical insight is that work can be performed in either of these two phases, which directly corresponds to the placement of the recursive call in your code:

* **Work in the Calling (Ascending) Phase:** This happens when the operation is performed **before** the recursive call. He connects this to the first Go example (`fun1`) and the first room analogy, stating, "if you see this recursion print and then call...bulbs were switched On while going."

* **Work in the Returning (Descending) Phase:** This happens when the operation is performed **after** the recursive call. This matches the second Go example (`fun2`) and the second room analogy. He explains, "in this example, first go to the next room. That is make a call and then print...bulbs were switched On while returning."

To provide a final, memorable image, he compares the process to a rubber band: "recursive functions are just like a rubber band if you stretch a rubber band pull it. So **next, next, next, next.** If you release, it will **come back.** It will come back, so same way recursive function, **call and call and call and then return, return.**"

He ends with a piece of advice: don't get confused by the literal output values (like `1,2,3` vs. `3,2,1`). The important thing is to "**just understand the procedure, how it is working**" and recognize whether the primary action happens during the calling phase or the returning phase.
