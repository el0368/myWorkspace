# Welcome! Is This Operating Systems Course for You?

### The Frustration is Real (And We're Here to Help)

Have you ever built an application, only to watch it suffer from performance issues or unpredictable behavior? It’s a common and frustrating experience for developers to feel lost, staring at a system that works like a "black box." You know your code is running, but you don't know _what's really going on_ underneath.

This course is designed to help you harness that frustration. We believe that curiosity born from real-world pain is the most powerful learning tool. We'll help you channel that energy to build a foundational knowledge of the OS, turning confusion into confidence.

### Who Should Take This Course?

#### The Ideal Learner Profile

- You are a **beginner-to-intermediate level developer**.
- You have experience **building backend applications**.
- You have professional software development experience and have **suffered the pain** of not knowing why your application is behaving a certain way.
- You are **curious** and want to transform your frustration into a deeper understanding of the systems you rely on every day.

#### A Note on Prerequisites

While this course covers beginner and intermediate concepts, we absolutely recommend that you have built some applications before. Having prior experience will make the concepts we discuss much more relatable, as you'll be able to connect them directly to the software challenges you've already faced.

If this sounds like you, here is a glimpse of the fundamental knowledge you will gain.

### Your Learning Journey: What We Will Demystify

Our primary goal is to provide you with a fundamental understanding of what's happening inside the OS. By the end of this course, you will be able to run your application and know with a "99% degree" of certainty what is _exactly_ happening under the hood.

#### Core Concepts We'll Uncover

- **Processes and Threads**
    - Learn the critical differences between these fundamental units of execution and understand what happens when you spawn a new process or thread.
- **CPU Scheduling**
    - Discover how the operating system works to achieve a _truly fair_ selection of which processes get to run on the CPU at any given time.
- **Context Switching**
    - Understand the "cost of doing business" associated with the kernel switching the CPU from one task to another, a key factor in system performance.
- **Storage and I/O**
    - Explore the world of Input/Output, a major source of innovation in modern kernels and a particular passion of your instructor. You'll understand why I/O performance is absolutely critical for high-performing backend systems and databases.
- **Memory Access & CPU Caching**
    - Demystify how your application accesses memory and how the system uses CPU caches to optimize performance.
- **Kernel Data Structures**
    - Gain a new appreciation for data structures and algorithms by seeing how the kernel uses them. This isn't academic; it's about _necessity_. We'll explore why specific data structures are chosen because they are the _only_ efficient solution for a given problem, a lesson that will change how you view your own code.

#### Our Guiding Philosophy

This course focuses on the **core concepts of the kernel**, which are applicable across almost all modern operating systems, including Windows and Linux. While different systems have their own ways of achieving the same goal, the fundamental principles remain the same.

We will cover the basics and intermediate levels in detail. We will also "brush over" some advanced topics to spark your curiosity and give you a foundation to pursue more specialized knowledge if you choose.

By the end, you'll not only understand these concepts but also see their direct impact on the software you write every day.

## Glossary of Key Terms

|   |   |
|---|---|
|Term|Definition|
|**Operating System (OS)**|The underlying system that developers often consider a "black box." The course aims to demystify its core concepts, which are similar across different OS implementations.|
|**Kernel**|The central focus of the course. It is the core of the OS, filled with essential data structures, and is responsible for managing processes, memory, and I/O.|
|**Process**|A fundamental OS concept. The course explains what a process is and covers the goal of fair selection and scheduling among processes.|
|**Thread**|A fundamental OS concept related to processes. The course covers what happens when a thread is created or is "spinning."|
|**Context Switching**|A specific, low-level kernel operation that the course will explain in detail.|
|**Data Structures**|Collections of data used extensively and out of necessity by the kernel for efficiency (e.g., linked lists). Understanding their use is a key learning outcome.|
|**I/O (Input/Output)**|A key area of OS functionality, particularly storage I/O. It is a specialty of the course creator and an area of recent innovation in kernel development.|
|**Scheduling**|The mechanism by which the kernel achieves the goal of "fair selection of processes."|
|**CPU (Central Processing Unit)**|The primary hardware component for computation. The course covers how the OS deals with the CPU and related topics like caching.|
|**CPU Caching**|A specific topic related to the CPU that the course will cover.|
