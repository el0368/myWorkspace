### Complexity in Different Data Structures 🏗️

The time it takes to do something depends on the **procedure** and the **data structure** you are using. Let's look at a few common ones in Go.

#### Matrix (Slice of Slices)

Imagine a grid, like a chessboard or a spreadsheet. In Go, we would represent this as a slice of slices (`[][]int`).1 If you have a square matrix that is `n` rows by `n` columns, what is the complexity of visiting every single cell?

- To do this, you need a `for` loop to go through each of the `n` rows.
    
- And _inside_ that loop, you need another `for` loop to go through each of the `n` columns in that row.
    

This is a classic nested loop. So, the time complexity to process the whole matrix is **$O(n^2)$**.

But what if you only want to process a single row? In that case, you are just looping through `n` items, so that would be **$O(n)$**.

---

#### Graph (Adjacency List)

This one is a little different and very interesting. Imagine a social network. You have people, and the connections between them. We can call the people "vertices" and the connections "edges."2

In Go, a common way to store this is with an adjacency list, which can be a slice of slices. You have an outer slice of length `n` (for `n` people). Each person's entry is another slice containing all the people they are connected to.3

Now, if you want to do work that involves visiting every person and following every single connection, the time it takes depends on two things:

- The number of people (`n`).
    
- The total number of connections (`m`).
    

So, the time complexity is **`$O(n + m)$`**. We need both parts to get the full story.

Think of it like throwing a party. What is the total cost? It depends on the **number of friends you invite (`n`)** AND the **total amount of food they all eat (`m`)**. You need both numbers for the full picture. It's the same idea here.

---

#### Binary Tree

A binary tree is a structure where each node can have up to two children, a left one and a right one.4 Think of it like a family tree that branches out.

Here, the complexity depends entirely on what you are doing:

- **Going down a single path:** If the tree is "balanced" (meaning it's not lopsided), and you want to go from the root at the top down to a leaf at the bottom, you are cutting the amount of work in half at each level.5 This is very fast! The time complexity is **`$O(log n)`**.
    
- **Visiting every node:** If your task is to visit every single node in the tree, then you have no choice but to touch all `n` of them. This is a "traversal," and its time complexity is **`$O(n)`**.
