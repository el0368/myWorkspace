### The Core Concept of Time Complexity ⏱️

The instructor begins by emphasizing that while the topic sounds intimidating, it's actually quite straightforward. In his words, "Though the word complexity is used, it is very simple."

The fundamental idea is to measure the amount of time required to complete a task. He relates this directly to daily life: **"when we do any work, any task, we want to know how much time it takes for...performing that particular task. Let us suppose, it is a one hour task, or a one day task."**

He then applies this same logic to the work done by computers. Just as we'd evaluate a machine in the real world, we must evaluate the efficiency of our programs. He uses an analogy: if a person can make bread in 20 minutes, but a machine takes 4-5 hours, "then it's better to do it manually. So, how much time a machine takes is very important for us."

This leads to the central point of his introduction: when we use computers to solve problems, we need a way to measure the time it takes. The most critical factor determining this time is not the computer's raw speed, but the method used to solve the problem. The instructor concludes that the time taken **"actually...depends on the process, or the procedure for completing that task. So, the Time Complexity basically depends on the procedure that you are adopting."**
