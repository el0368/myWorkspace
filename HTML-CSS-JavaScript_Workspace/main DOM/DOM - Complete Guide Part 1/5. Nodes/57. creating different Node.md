### The Core Concept: "Node" is the Generic Term

Think of it like items in a grocery cart 🛒. You have different kinds of items—fruits, vegetables, drinks—but they are all generically called "items."

In the DOM:

- **Element Nodes** (`<h1>`, `<p>`) are like the apples and bread.
    
- **Text Nodes** (`"Hello World"`) are like the labels on the products.
    
- **Comment Nodes** (``) are like your shopping list that you tossed into the cart.
    

They are all different, but they are all classified as "nodes."

---

### Key Takeaways from His Demonstration

1. **There Are Different Node Types:** He showed this by creating an `<h1>` (Element Node), raw text outside of any tag (Text Node), and an HTML comment (Comment Node) to prove they all exist together in the DOM.
    
2. **The #1 Rule:** He reinforced the most important rule: **If it's written in your HTML file, it must be represented in the DOM.** This is why even invisible things like comments and the `<!DOCTYPE>` declaration at the top of your file become nodes. The DOM must be a complete model.
    
3. **JavaScript Can Access Anything:** Because everything is a node, JavaScript can theoretically access and read anything in your HTML file, including comments.
    

In short, he was showing you that the DOM is a literal tree made up of different types of nodes, and "node" is simply the universal name for any single piece of that tree.
