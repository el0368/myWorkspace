He was demonstrating the crucial difference between how two types of DOM collections behave when the page is changed:

- An **`HTMLCollection`** is **live** and updates automatically.1
    
- A **`NodeList`** (from `querySelectorAll`) is **static** and does not.2
    

---

### The Analogy: A Live Camera Feed vs. a Photograph

This is the easiest way to understand the difference:

- **`HTMLCollection` (Live) 📹:** Think of this as watching a **live security camera feed** of a bookshelf. If someone walks up and adds a new book, you see it on your monitor instantly. The feed is always showing the current reality.
    
- **`NodeList` (Static) 📸:** Think of this as **taking a photograph** of the same bookshelf. The photo captures the state of the shelf at that exact moment. If someone later adds a new book, your photo will **not** change. To see the current state, you would have to take a new photo.
    

---

### What He Demonstrated

The lecturer proved this concept with two experiments:

#### 1. The `HTMLCollection` Experiment (Live)

1. He used `getElementsByTagName('li')` to get an `HTMLCollection` of the 3 list items.
    
2. He then programmatically added a 4th `<li>` to the DOM.
    
3. When he checked the _original_ collection variable again, it **automatically updated** and now contained 4 items. This is the "live feed" behavior.
    

#### 2. The `NodeList` Experiment (Static)

1. He used `querySelectorAll('li')` to get a `NodeList` of the 3 list items.
    
2. He then programmatically added a 4th `<li>` to the DOM.
    
3. When he checked the _original_ `NodeList` variable, it **did not change**. It still only contained the original 3 items. This is the "photograph" behavior.
    

To see the new item, he had to "take a new photo" by running `querySelectorAll('li')` a second time.

### Why It Matters

This is an advanced topic, but it's important because it affects how your code behaves. Sometimes you want a list that always reflects the current state of the page (live). Other times, you need a stable, predictable list that won't change while you're working on it (static). Knowing the difference helps you avoid bugs and choose the right tool for the job.
