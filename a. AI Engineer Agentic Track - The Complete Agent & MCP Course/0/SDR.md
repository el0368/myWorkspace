In the context of business and the AI agent explanation I just gave, **SDR** stands for **Sales Development Representative**.1

While an "Account Executive" (AE) is the person who actually closes a deal and signs the contract, the SDR is the "hunter" or "scout" who does the hard work of finding potential customers in the first place.2

### What does an SDR actually do?

Their job is focused entirely on the **top of the sales funnel**.3 Their main responsibilities include:

1. **Prospecting:** Researching companies and people who might need the product.4
    
2. **Outreach:** Sending hundreds of cold emails, making cold calls, and messaging people on LinkedIn.5
    
3. **Qualification:** Talking to a lead to see if they actually have the money (**B**udget), the power to say yes (**A**uthority), a real problem to solve (**N**eed), and a deadline (**T**imeline).6 This is often called the **BANT** framework.
    
4. **Booking Meetings:** Their ultimate "win" is getting a qualified prospect to agree to a demo or a meeting with an Account Executive.7
    

---

### Why is this relevant to AI Agents?

The SDR role is one of the most popular targets for the **AI Agent revolution** because it is high-volume and highly repetitive.8

- **The "Grind":** A human SDR might spend 6 hours a day researching LinkedIn profiles and writing "personalized" emails.
    
- **The AI SDR:** An AI agent can research 1,000 prospects in seconds, read their recent news or tweets, write a perfectly personalized email to each one, and handle the back-and-forth scheduling if they reply—all while the human sleeps.
    

### SDR vs. BDR (Business Development Representative)

You will often see these terms used together. While they vary by company:

- **SDRs** usually handle **Inbound** leads (people who clicked an ad or signed up for a webinar).9
    
- **BDRs** usually handle **Outbound** leads (cold-contacting people who have never heard of the company).10
