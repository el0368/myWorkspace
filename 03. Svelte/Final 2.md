To make your "Curriculum Factory" functional, you need libraries that handle three specific areas: **Visualizing** (Svelte), **Calculating** (Elixir/Rust), and **Generating** (Python).

Here is the essential math toolkit for the **SPELP** stack:

---

### 1. The Visualization Layer (Svelte 5)

Since the student interacts with the math here, these libraries must be fast and reactive.

- **KaTeX:** The fastest library for rendering LaTeX math formulas. It is much lighter than MathJax and works perfectly inside Svelte runes.
    
- **Three.js / Threlte:** For your **3D math islands**. Threlte is a Svelte-specific wrapper for Three.js that makes building 3D coordinate systems feel like writing HTML.
    
- **Rough.js:** If you want your 2D geometry diagrams to have a "hand-drawn" look (like a teacher's whiteboard), this is the standard.
    
- **Function-Plot:** A powerful library built on top of D3 specifically for rendering 2D functions ($y = x^2$, etc.) with zooming and panning.
    

---

### 2. The Logic & Calculation Layer (Elixir + Rustler)

Elixir is the "Manager," but for heavy number crunching, we use **Rustler** to bring a tiny bit of Rust back purely for speed.

- **Nx (Numerical Elixir):** The gold standard for math in Elixir. It supports tensors and multi-dimensional arrays (like NumPy in Python). It’s perfect for curriculum mapping logic.
    
- **Rustler + nalgebra:** If you need to perform high-speed 3D linear algebra (matrix multiplications) on the backend, use the **nalgebra** crate inside a Rustler module. It keeps the "Unbreakable" Elixir shell but uses Rust's raw math power.
    
- **Decimal:** For financial math or high-precision decimals where floating-point errors (like $0.1 + 0.2 \neq 0.3$) are unacceptable.
    

---

### 3. The Generation Layer (Python AI Agents)

This is where your **Agentic AI** creates the exercises.

- **SymPy:** Essential. It is a Python library for symbolic mathematics. It can solve equations, simplify expressions, and—most importantly—output **LaTeX strings** that you can send to Elixir and then to Svelte.
    
- **NumPy / SciPy:** The industry standard for any data-heavy calculations your agents need to perform to verify if a generated math problem is actually solvable.
    
- **Pylatexenc:** To help your Python agents clean up and format the LaTeX they generate before sending it over the bridge to Elixir.
    

---

### Summary: Math Tooling Flow

|**Task**|**Library**|**Language**|
|---|---|---|
|**Equation Display**|KaTeX|Svelte|
|**3D Geometry**|Threlte (Three.js)|Svelte|
|**Function Plotting**|Function-Plot|Svelte|
|**Solving Equations**|SymPy|Python|
|**High-Speed Matrix Math**|nalgebra (via Rustler)|Elixir/Rust|
|**Tensor Logic**|Nx|Elixir|

---

### Why this works with SPELP

Because Svelte 5 uses **Runes**, libraries like `Threlte` and `KaTeX` will update the screen instantly as soon as the Elixir backend pushes new data. Your **Python agent** generates a formula in `SymPy`, sends the LaTeX string to **Elixir**, which then "flows" it into the **Svelte** island where `KaTeX` renders it perfectly.
