### Part 5 Vocabularies: Mathematical Operations

1. **Addition Operator (`+`)**:
    
    - **Definition**: The symbol used to perform the mathematical operation of addition.
        
    - **Function**: Combines two numerical values to find their sum.
        
    - **Example**: `5 + 3` results in `8`.
        
2. **Subtraction Operator (`-`)**:
    
    - **Definition**: The symbol used to perform the mathematical operation of subtraction.
        
    - **Function**: Finds the difference between two numerical values.
        
    - **Example**: `10 - 4` results in `6`.
        
3. **Multiplication Operator (`*`)**:
    
    - **Definition**: The asterisk symbol (`*`) used to perform the mathematical operation of multiplication.
        
    - **Function**: Finds the product of two numerical values.
        
    - **Example**: `2 * 6` results in `12`.
        
4. **Division Operator (`/`)**:
    
    - **Definition**: The forward slash symbol (`/`) used to perform the mathematical operation of division.
        
    - **Function**: Divides one numerical value by another.
        
    - **Key Behavior (Floor Division for Integers)**: When both operands are **integers**, Rust performs **floor division**, meaning it truncates (chops off) any decimal component, returning only the whole number part. To get a decimal result, at least one of the operands (and typically both) must be a floating-point number.
        
    - **Example**:
        
        - `10 / 3` (integer division) results in `3`.
            
        - `10.0 / 3.0` (floating-point division) results in `3.333...`.
            
5. **Modulo Operator (`%`)**:
    
    - **Definition**: The percentage sign symbol (`%`) used to perform the modulo operation.
        
    - **Function**: Returns the **remainder** after one numerical value is divided by another.
        
    - **Example**: `10 % 3` results in `1` (because 10 divided by 3 is 3 with a remainder of 1). `15 % 4` results in `3`.
        
6. **Floor Division**:
    
    - **Definition**: A specific type of division performed when dividing one integer by another in Rust. It means that any fractional or decimal part of the result is **discarded or truncated**, and only the whole number part is returned.
        
    - **Relevance**: This is the default behavior of the `/` operator when both operands are integers.
        
    - **Example**: `7 / 2` (integer floor division) results in `3`, not `3.5`.
        
7. **Augmented Assignment Operators**:
    
    - **Definition**: A set of operators that combine a mathematical operation (like addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, or modulo) with the assignment (`=`) operator.
        
    - **Function**: They perform the specified mathematical operation on a variable's current value, and then immediately assign the result back to that same variable.
        
    - **Requirement**: The variable being operated on **must be declared as mutable (`mut`)** because its original value is being changed.
        
    - **Common Examples**:
        
        - `+=` (add and assign): `x += 5;` is equivalent to `x = x + 5;`
            
        - `-=` (subtract and assign): `x -= 2;` is equivalent to `x = x - 2;`
            
        - `*=` (multiply and assign): `x *= 3;` is equivalent to `x = x * 3;`
            
        - `/=` (divide and assign): `x /= 2;` is equivalent to `x = x / 2;`
            
        - `%=` (modulo and assign): `x %= 3;` is equivalent to `x = x % 3;`
            
    - **Example**:
        
        Rust
        
        ```
        let mut score = 50;
        score += 10; // score is now 60
        score *= 2;  // score is now 120
        ```
